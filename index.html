Professional Self-Assessment

I am a computer science professional preparing to apply my technical education and process safety management experience to a career in STEM project management. My work combines structured problem-solving, attention to risk, and a commitment to clear communication.

Throughout my Bachelor of Science in Computer Science program at Southern New Hampshire University, I have developed practical experience in mobile application development, software architecture, algorithms, database design, and secure programming. My work on the CS-465 Travlr Getaways full-stack application strengthened my understanding of how user interfaces, APIs, and data layers interact. In CS-360, I developed an Android Weight Tracker application, and in CS-370, I explored reinforcement learning through the Treasure Hunt intelligent agent.

My professional experience as a Process Safety Management Coordinator at Tyson Foods has reinforced the importance of documentation, identifying risks early, working with stakeholders, and following through on technical decisions. These habits carry into my approach to software development: understand the problem, consider constraints and trade-offs, implement a solution, and evaluate the evidence.

My capstone demonstrates five connected professional competencies: collaborating and making sound decisions; communicating technical information to varied audiences; evaluating algorithms and design trade-offs; applying appropriate computing tools and techniques; and building a security-focused mindset that protects information and considers potential vulnerabilities.

The three enhancements below show my growth across software engineering and design, algorithms and data structures, and databases. Together, they demonstrate my ability to revisit existing work, identify meaningful improvements, and document both accomplishments and limitations. I intend to carry these skills into STEM project management, where successful outcomes depend on technical understanding, responsible decision-making, and effective communication.
